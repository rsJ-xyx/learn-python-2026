A software agent is a program that takes actions on behalf of a user. Unlike a
simple chat model, an agent can plan, call tools, observe results, and decide
what to do next. Agents work well when the task is open ended, when the steps
are not known in advance, and when a wrong answer is easy to detect and correct.
They work badly when the task is fixed and simple, because every extra step adds
cost, latency, and a chance to fail.

The most useful pattern is small and boring: give the model a clear goal, a small
set of well described tools, and a way to check whether it succeeded. Then let it
loop until the goal is met or the budget runs out. Most production failures come
not from weak models but from vague goals, overlapping tools, and no feedback.

Retrieval is the second half of the story. A model that can look things up is
more accurate than one that only remembers. Split the documents into chunks,
embed them, store the vectors, and search when a question arrives. Reranking and
citation checking turn a plausible answer into a trustworthy one.

Good engineering here is mostly about measurement. Build a small test set,
measure retrieval recall, measure task success, count the money you spend, and
read the traces when something breaks. Everything else is decoration.
